# Source-label and publication context brief

The supervised targets are **operational differences between matched DR1 source products**, rather than calibrated measurements of physical dust attenuation. This distinction remains necessary even if held-out prediction errors are small. Four previously inspected galaxies with exactly zero projected dust fields exhibit a coherent g-band pair offset of approximately **0.12 mag**, corresponding to about **10.6% lower dusty-product flux**. This is not adequately described as a noise-only floor.

Independent decoding of 24 original O1 g/J/Ks images yielded exact equality with the earlier cached crops. All 12 clear/dusty pair headers agree in units and coordinates, and all four freshly decoded O1 dust maps contain zero nonzero pixels. The g offset remains over the complete 160-kpc field: median +0.120994 mag, range +0.117605 to +0.123231 across four galaxies and five views each. These 20 views are repeated measurements, not 20 independent control galaxies. Across the 22 source bands, all 20 views retain each band's sign, but signs alternate between bands: for example r −0.023176 mag, z −0.052064 mag and FUV −0.152646 mag. This pattern disfavors a simple extinction-law explanation or an aperture/cache/unit error as the sole cause.

There is also a spatial component compatible with Monte Carlo noise: after flux normalization, median g shape-residual RMS relative to clear RMS falls from 0.0181 to 0.0056 with 1-kpc-FWHM smoothing. The corresponding Ks change is 0.0203 to 0.0069. Smoothing sensitivity supports a fluctuating component, while coherent integrated offsets require further investigation. [Baes et al. 2024 DR1 methodology](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04224v1) describes analytic clear-source projections and Monte Carlo dusty broadband instruments. Current official SKIRT documentation distinguishes a probe's default monochromatic sampling from optional band convolution, providing a specific candidate mechanism for an SED-dependent operator difference. Historical DR1 configuration/filter versions were unavailable, so this candidate is **not an established cause**. [Official probe definition](https://skirt.ugent.be/skirt9/class_imported_source_luminosity_probe.html).

Keep signed labels; do not subtract a universal 0.12-mag offset. The old fixed-R12 maximum absolute control values—0.1239 mag in g and 0.0216 mag in Ks—are descriptive empirical envelopes from four galaxies, not standard deviations, physical detection thresholds or transferable confidence bounds. Adaptive-aperture envelopes must be remeasured with the final measurement operator, and cannot inherit native fixed-R12 thresholds. Source-null controls should be shown separately from primary positive-dust performance. Physical-label systematic uncertainty is not included in predictive residual intervals.

Publication claims should acknowledge direct precedents. [Fu et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/540/3/2081/8140852) already predicts dust attenuation from high-redshift simulated JWST images, with observational SED comparisons rather than direct dust truth. The closest verified resolved-dust mapping reference is [Dobbels & Baes **2021**, A&A 655 A34](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01704), not a uniquely identified “Dobbels 2022” paper. [Bokona Tulu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20886v1) directly measures wavelength and dust effects on nonparametric morphology using the same 1154-galaxy TSA population; broad claims that TSA dust-induced morphology has not previously been studied are untenable. Sim-to-real transfer remains a separate validation task, as illustrated by [DeepMerge II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01373).

A defensible contribution is a grouped-galaxy test of whether observable photometry and spatial structure predict the declared source-product biases beyond simple baselines, with explicit uncertainty, control and domain limits. Adaptive “optical” models are conditional on a Ks-selected reference aperture; fixed R12 provides a cleaner g-only aperture control. Report paired galaxy-bootstrap intervals as conditional on the fitted models, and calibration coverage separately for each target, without claiming joint coverage across all eight outputs.
